Carbon math

April 7, 2020

These circles represent the amount of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere that is above the safe level for life on Earth ("carbon debt"), the amount that is added every year at current emissions levels ("annual emissions"), and the IPCC estimate of annual carbon removal required by 2050 to keep global warming under 1.5°C above the stable pre-industrial global average temperature, assuming dramatic, near total emissions cuts also occur by 2050 ("hypothetical removal").

Carbon debt

Research has shown 350ppm (~1°C) is likely to be a safe and stable level of warming. We are currently above this safe level, at about 413ppm.

Put another way, there are currently at least 945 billion tons more of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere than is safe for life on Earth. Nearly a trillion tons of carbon dioxide would need to be removed from the atmosphere to get back to a safe and stable level of warming.

Sources: James Hansen, NASA.

~ 945 Gt

Annual emissions

Global annual emissions from human activity were about 36 Gt in 2019. This does not include biosphere emissions, such as permafrost thaw and wildfires.

With biotic emissions included, global annual emissions were about 42 Gt in 2019. In this visual representation, the size of the outer circle represents 42 Gt.

Source: Our World in Data.

~ 36 Gt

Hypothetical removal

The IPCC estimate of annual carbon removal required by 2050 to keep global warming under 1.5°C over the long term, in conjunction with reaching near-zero human emissions, is 10 Gt per year.

10 Gt

Annual removal on this scale is historically unprecedented. If we accomplish it, and dramatically slash emissions to near zero by 2050, the IPCC expects we can limit global warming to under 1.5°C over the long term.

Source: IPCC.

Why make this?

I made this because there were no signs global emissions were slowing and I got interested enough in carbon removal to host a monthly lecture series called Carbon Removal NYC. Scientists working on novel carbon removal technologies and enhanced biological techniques spoke to a public climate-curious audience about their work. The approaches they described were exciting and made me feel hopeful. This led me to wonder: What is the potential impact of carbon removal on warming? How much hope should we give it?

I wanted to understand how ppm, annual emissions, carbon removal aspirations, and warming level goals are related. How much more CO2 is in the atmosphere than should be there? At what rate are we adding more? How much do people hope to remove? And what is the relative scale between all three of these? Ultimately, I wanted to know: How realistic are goals to stay under 1.5°C or 2°C of warming, and can carbon removal play a meaningful part in achieving these goals?

This "carbon math" exercise led me to conclude: not realistic, and no.

Methodology

The math for the circles is based on the sources below and a back-of-envelop formula for converting gigatonnes (Gt) of CO2 emissions into the atmosphere to ppm: 1 ppm ≈ 15Gt CO2 emissions (7.5Gt CO2 remains in the air; 7.5Gt CO2 absorbed by the ocean and biosphere). The formula is described by Klaus Lackner (on slide 7). Please note that this formula assumes the ocean and biosphere continue to absorb carbon dioxide at the same rate, which is likely not to be the case at higher levels of warming. I would welcome advice and corrections and additional sources.

The circles are simple pixel math. After the ppm and CO2 calculations, I took the square root of the results to calculate a diameter for the circles and used CSS to represent them in relative terms to each other, with pixels as the unit.
